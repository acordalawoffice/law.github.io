    <h3 style="font-size: 2.0rem; color: #1a202c; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 10px;">When a Wage Hike Squeezes the Pay Gap: Understanding Wage Distortion</h3>
    <div class="article-meta" style="font-size: 0.85rem; color: #718096; margin-bottom: 20px;">Published on November 14, 2026 &bull; Legal Educational Article</div>

    <div class="article-body" style="color: #2d3748; line-height: 1.7; text-align: justify;">
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">Imagine a supervisor who has worked for the same company for twelve years. A new wage order takes effect, and the trainee who joined last month now earns a basic salary very close to the supervisor's. The supervisor has the experience, the responsibility, and the loyalty, yet the pay gap that once recognized all of that has nearly disappeared. In Philippine labor law, this kind of situation may amount to wage distortion.</p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 20px;">A wage increase ordered by the government is meant to help workers at the bottom of the pay scale, and it does. However, if nothing is done for the employees above them, the company's pay structure can be thrown off balance. This article explains what wage distortion is, how it is corrected, and what may happen when an employer ignores it.</p>

        <h4 style="font-size: 1.15rem; color: #2d3748; margin-top: 25px; margin-bottom: 12px;">What Is Wage Distortion?</h4>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">Under Article 124 of the Labor Code, wage distortion occurs when an increase in prescribed wage rates eliminates or severely reduces the intentional pay differences between employee groups, effectively destroying the distinctions in the company's wage structure that were based on skills, length of service, or other legitimate reasons <span style="font-size: 0.92em; color: #4a5568;">(Article 124 of the Labor Code, as amended by Republic Act No. 6727; restated in NWPC Advisory No. 01, Series of 2023)</span>.</p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">In simple terms, the problem arises when a government-mandated wage increase substantially erases the pay gap that was deliberately maintained between different levels of employees.</p>

        <div style="margin: 20px 0; padding: 15px 20px; background: #f7fafc; border-left: 4px solid #2b6cb0; border-radius: 4px;">
            <p style="margin: 0; font-size: 0.95rem;"><strong>Remember:</strong> Not every salary gap is a wage distortion. The issue arises when a government-mandated wage increase eliminates or severely contracts intentional pay differences within an established wage structure. The cause of the change in pay matters just as much as the resulting gap.</p>
        </div>

        <h4 style="font-size: 1.15rem; color: #2d3748; margin-top: 25px; margin-bottom: 12px;">How Does It Happen?</h4>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">It usually begins with a wage order. When a new wage order takes effect, employees covered by the order whose wages fall below the newly prescribed minimum must receive the required adjustment. However, employees who already earn above the new minimum do not automatically receive the same increase, unless the applicable wage order provides otherwise. As a result, the wage gap between lower-paid and higher-paid employee groups may shrink, potentially creating wage distortion.</p>

        <h4 style="font-size: 1.15rem; color: #2d3748; margin-top: 25px; margin-bottom: 12px;">How Do You Know If There Is a Wage Distortion?</h4>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">The Supreme Court identified four elements of wage distortion in <em>Prubankers Association v. Prudential Bank and Trust Company</em>, G.R. No. 131247, January 25, 1999, and the National Wages and Productivity Commission (NWPC) further clarified them in NWPC Advisory No. 01, Series of 2023 (August 14, 2023):</p>
        <ol style="margin-bottom: 15px; padding-left: 25px;">
            <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">There is an existing ladder of positions, each with its own pay rate.</li>
            <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">The pay of a lower position goes up significantly, while the higher position does not receive a matching increase.</li>
            <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">The distinction between the two levels is eliminated or severely reduced.</li>
            <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">The distortion is limited to positions under the same employer in the same region.</li>
        </ol>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">On the third point, the Supreme Court has explained that the law does not require the pay gap to disappear completely, because a severe contraction is enough <span style="font-size: 0.92em; color: #4a5568;">(<em>Metropolitan Bank and Trust Company Employees Union-ALU-TUCP v. NLRC</em>, G.R. No. 102636, September 10, 1993)</span>. In that case, the Court considered a contraction of approximately 83 percent, first identified in the dissenting opinion of an NLRC commissioner, to be plainly severe. The NWPC adds that "elimination" means the old pay gap no longer exists, while "severe contraction" means a reduction of more than 50 percent of the intended pay difference <span style="font-size: 0.92em; color: #4a5568;">(NWPC Advisory No. 01, Series of 2023, which sets out the 50 percent computation; and NWPC educational material, <em>Correcting Wage Distortion</em> (2026))</span>. For example, if the original gap between two positions was ₱80 a day and it becomes ₱30, the gap has contracted by 62.5 percent. This 50 percent benchmark is an NWPC guideline stated in those materials, not a numerical threshold written into Article 124.</p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">The comparison must also be made among positions under the same employer in the same region <span style="font-size: 0.92em; color: #4a5568;">(<em>Prubankers Association v. Prudential Bank and Trust Company</em>, G.R. No. 131247, January 25, 1999; as clarified in NWPC Advisory No. 01, Series of 2023)</span>. Different regional minimum wage rates do not, by themselves, create wage distortion simply because employees holding comparable positions in different regions receive different salaries.</p>

        <h4 style="font-size: 1.15rem; color: #2d3748; margin-top: 25px; margin-bottom: 12px;">What Is Not a Wage Distortion?</h4>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">Compare two situations. In the first, a wage order raises the pay of a lower position and substantially erases the intended gap with a higher position. That may be a wage distortion. In the second, an employer hires a new employee at a higher starting salary than an existing employee because market rates have gone up. That is not automatically a wage distortion, even though the result may feel just as unfair to the existing employee.</p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">The reason is that the compulsory correction under Article 124 applies to distortions resulting from wage increases required by law or by a wage order <span style="font-size: 0.92em; color: #4a5568;">(Article 124 of the Labor Code, as amended by Republic Act No. 6727; <em>Bankard Employees Union-Workers Alliance Trade Unions v. NLRC</em>, G.R. No. 140689, February 17, 2004)</span>. As a general rule, it does not apply merely because an employer voluntarily changes its salary structure, such as by offering higher starting salaries to attract new hires. Nor does a negotiated salary increase, by itself, constitute a legal wage distortion under Article 124. However, a wage differential intentionally established through a collective bargaining agreement may still be protected when a subsequent government-mandated wage increase substantially reduces or eliminates it <span style="font-size: 0.92em; color: #4a5568;">(<em>Metropolitan Bank and Trust Company Employees Union-ALU-TUCP v. NLRC</em>, G.R. No. 102636, September 10, 1993)</span>.</p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">The Supreme Court describes the first kind of situation as a "legal" wage distortion and the second as a "factual" one <span style="font-size: 0.92em; color: #4a5568;">(<em>Mindanao International Container Terminal Services, Inc. v. MICTSILU-FDLO</em>, G.R. No. 245918, November 29, 2022)</span>. A factual wage distortion does not, by itself, oblige the employer to correct it unless some other source of obligation requires it, such as a law, a collective bargaining agreement, or an established company practice <span style="font-size: 0.92em; color: #4a5568;">(<em>Bankard Employees Union-Workers Alliance Trade Unions v. NLRC</em>, G.R. No. 140689, February 17, 2004)</span>.</p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">This does not mean that employers have unlimited freedom to impose different salaries on employees performing substantially equal work. Salary differences must still be supported by reasonable and legitimate criteria, such as differences in qualifications, skills, work experience, seniority, length of service, or performance-based incentives, and the Supreme Court has said that it is the employer who must show those reasons <span style="font-size: 0.92em; color: #4a5568;">(<em>Mindanao International Container Terminal Services, Inc. v. MICTSILU-FDLO</em>, G.R. No. 245918, November 29, 2022)</span>. The fact that a difference is not a wage distortion does not automatically make it lawful under every other labor-law principle, including the principle of equal pay for equal work.</p>

        <h4 style="font-size: 1.15rem; color: #2d3748; margin-top: 25px; margin-bottom: 12px;">Why Does It Matter?</h4>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">For employees, a squeezed pay gap can feel like years of effort and loyalty no longer count. For employers, the effects are just as real. Morale drops, experienced employees may start looking elsewhere, and resentment can build between groups of workers. In a unionized workplace, it can also strain relations with the union.</p>

        <h4 style="font-size: 1.15rem; color: #2d3748; margin-top: 25px; margin-bottom: 12px;">How Is It Corrected?</h4>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">The law encourages employers and employees to settle the matter by talking to each other first.</p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;"><strong>In a workplace with a union:</strong> The employer and the union negotiate to correct the distortion. If they cannot agree, the dispute goes through the grievance procedure under their collective bargaining agreement. If it is still unresolved, it proceeds to voluntary arbitration, where a neutral person chosen by both sides decides the matter. Unless the parties agree otherwise in writing, the voluntary arbitrator must decide the dispute within ten calendar days from the time it is referred to voluntary arbitration <span style="font-size: 0.92em; color: #4a5568;">(Article 124 of the Labor Code, as amended by Republic Act No. 6727; see also NWPC Advisory No. 01, Series of 2023, paragraph C.1)</span>.</p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;"><strong>In a workplace with no collective bargaining agreement or recognized labor union:</strong> The employer and the employees should first endeavor to correct the distortion themselves. Under Article 124 of the Labor Code, as amended by Republic Act No. 6727, any unresolved dispute is referred to the National Conciliation and Mediation Board (NCMB) for conciliation. If it remains unresolved after ten calendar days of conciliation, the law provides for referral to the appropriate branch of the National Labor Relations Commission (NLRC), which must conduct continuous hearings and decide within twenty calendar days from the time the dispute is submitted for compulsory arbitration. NWPC Advisory No. 01, Series of 2023 (paragraph C.2) and the later NWPC educational material, <em>Correcting Wage Distortion</em> (2026) describe the administrative process in terms of mandatory conciliation-mediation under the NCMB's Single-Entry Approach. If no settlement is reached, the matter may be resolved through a labor arbiter, or both parties may agree to submit it to voluntary arbitration.</p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">Two more points are worth remembering. First, a dispute involving wage distortion cannot be used as a ground for declaring a strike or a lockout. The law provides peaceful mechanisms of negotiation, conciliation, and arbitration instead. Second, the pendency of a wage distortion dispute does not delay the implementation of the wage increase prescribed by law or by the wage order. An employer cannot hold back the mandated increase while the discussions are still going on <span style="font-size: 0.92em; color: #4a5568;">(Article 124 of the Labor Code, as amended by Republic Act No. 6727; NWPC educational material, <em>Correcting Wage Distortion</em> (2026))</span>.</p>

        <h4 style="font-size: 1.15rem; color: #2d3748; margin-top: 25px; margin-bottom: 12px;">Is There an Official Formula?</h4>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">Article 124 does not prescribe one universal formula for correcting wage distortion. The objective is to restore the meaningful distinctions in the existing wage structure that were substantially reduced or eliminated by the mandated increase <span style="font-size: 0.92em; color: #4a5568;">(Article 124 of the Labor Code, as amended by Republic Act No. 6727; NWPC Advisory No. 01, Series of 2023)</span>.</p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">The NWPC issued Advisory No. 01, Series of 2023 (August 14, 2023), which lists several suggested approaches: the Pineda Formula, the Pineda-Cruz-So Formula, the Percentile Approach, the Philippine Construction Supply Formula, the JODA Formula, the Wirerope Formula, and the Bagtas Approach. These are guides rather than mandatory formulas, and the NWPC itself notes that employers and workers may agree on a formula that fits their own circumstances. The advisory also points out that some approaches suit particular situations. For example, the Percentile Approach is meant for large groups of employees with no clear job and pay structure, the Wirerope Formula credits increases already given under a collective bargaining agreement, and the JODA Formula is best for small pay differences. The appropriate method depends on the company's salary structure, the extent of the distortion, the applicable wage order, and the agreement or decision reached through the proper process.</p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">To make the idea concrete, here is a simplified illustration using the Pineda Formula described in NWPC Advisory No. 01, Series of 2023, which gives smaller adjustments to employees who are paid further above the old minimum. The numbers are made up. Suppose the old daily minimum wage was ₱600, and a wage order adds ₱50, making the new minimum ₱650. An employee who earned ₱680 a day before the wage order was ₱80 above the old minimum, but is now only ₱30 above the new minimum, a reduction of 62.5 percent in the original gap.</p>
        <ul style="margin-bottom: 15px; padding-left: 25px;">
            <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">The formula multiplies the mandated increase by the old minimum wage divided by the employee's existing daily wage before the wage order: ₱50 &times; (₱600 &divide; ₱680), which is about ₱44.12.</li>
            <li style="margin-bottom: 8px;">The employee's adjusted rate is therefore about ₱724.12 a day, which is roughly ₱74 above the new minimum.</li>
        </ul>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">Other formulas would produce different amounts for the same facts, and the parties may agree on another reasonable adjustment. This example is only a simplified illustration of one possible approach. It is not a result prescribed or endorsed by law. Notably, in <em>Metropolitan Bank and Trust Company Employees Union-ALU-TUCP v. NLRC</em>, G.R. No. 102636, September 10, 1993, the Supreme Court rejected an across-the-board increase and instead adopted the formula proposed by the dissenting NLRC commissioner to correct the wage distortion.</p>

        <h4 style="font-size: 1.15rem; color: #2d3748; margin-top: 25px; margin-bottom: 12px;">What If the Employer Does Nothing?</h4>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">Ignoring a genuine wage distortion is risky, although the consequences are not automatic. If the parties cannot resolve the matter, the dispute may eventually be decided through voluntary or compulsory arbitration, as the case may be <span style="font-size: 0.92em; color: #4a5568;">(Article 124 of the Labor Code, as amended by Republic Act No. 6727; NWPC Advisory No. 01, Series of 2023)</span>. If a wage distortion is established, the proper tribunal may determine the adjustments needed to correct it, including any salary differences properly due to the affected employees. The final outcome depends on the facts, the company's wage structure, and how the adjustment is computed.</p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">Even before that point, the company bears the cost of unhappy employees, lost talent, and a strained workplace. Most of the time, a sincere and early conversation costs far less than a dispute.</p>

        <h4 style="font-size: 1.15rem; color: #2d3748; margin-top: 25px; margin-bottom: 12px;">Practical Tips</h4>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;"><strong>For employers:</strong> Review your pay structure each time a new wage order takes effect. Compare the basic pay of different positions before and after the increase, talk to your employees early, and keep a written record of the discussions and the agreement reached. The NWPC also encourages employers to seek guidance and technical assistance from the Regional Tripartite Wages and Productivity Boards <span style="font-size: 0.92em; color: #4a5568;">(NWPC Advisory No. 01, Series of 2023; NWPC educational material, <em>Correcting Wage Distortion</em> (2026))</span>.</p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;"><strong>For employees:</strong> Check whether the gap between your basic pay and that of the position below you has truly shrunk because of a wage order. If it has, raise the matter politely with your employer, or through your union if you have one. Keep your payslips and pay records.</p>

        <h4 style="font-size: 1.15rem; color: #2d3748; margin-top: 25px; margin-bottom: 12px;">A Final Word</h4>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 20px;">A mandated wage increase should not unnecessarily erase the meaningful distinctions in an established pay structure. When employers and employees approach wage distortion with openness and fairness, they can protect both the interests of workers and the stability of the workplace. If you are unsure whether a particular situation amounts to wage distortion, it is worth examining the company's salary structure and the applicable wage order before the issue grows.</p>
