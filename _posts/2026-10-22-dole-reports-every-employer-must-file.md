    <h3 style="font-size: 2.0rem; color: #1a202c; margin-top: 0; margin-bottom: 10px;">DOLE Reports Every Philippine Employer Should Know: What to File, When, and Why</h3>
    <div class="article-meta" style="font-size: 0.85rem; color: #718096; margin-bottom: 20px;">Published on October 22, 2026 &bull; Legal Educational Article</div>

    <div class="article-body" style="color: #2d3748; line-height: 1.7; text-align: justify;">
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">Picture this: a DOLE inspector arrives at your workplace, greets you politely, and asks, &ldquo;May I see your reports?&rdquo; For many business owners and HR managers, that is the moment they realize they are not sure which reports they were supposed to file in the first place.</p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">The good news is that most DOLE reports follow a predictable rhythm. Some are due every month, some once a year, and some only when something specific happens, like a workplace accident or a closure. Once you know the rhythm, staying on top of it is far easier than it looks.</p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 20px;">A quick note before we start: the workplace safety reports below are governed by the Revised Implementing Rules and Regulations of the Occupational Safety and Health Law (DOLE Department Order No. 252, Series of 2025, in effect since May 2025). DOLE updates its advisories and online portals from time to time, so it is always wise to confirm the latest instructions with your DOLE Regional Office.</p>

        <h4 style="font-size: 1.15rem; color: #2d3748; margin-top: 25px; margin-bottom: 12px;">Every Month: The Work Accident/Illness Report</h4>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">This report tells DOLE about workplace accidents and work-related illnesses. Under the revised safety rules and DOLE Labor Advisory No. 07, Series of 2022, employers submit it every 30th of the month through the DOLE Online Compliance Portal or the DOLE Regional, Provincial, or Field Office that covers the workplace. Here is the part many employers miss: you still have to file even in months when no accident, illness, or disabling injury happened. If you use contractors, the principal prepares the report and must make sure the contractors&rsquo; workers deployed at the workplace are identified in it. This does not remove the contractor&rsquo;s own safety duties toward its workers.</p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">Serious incidents cannot wait for month-end. Under DOLE Department Order No. 252, Series of 2025, a dangerous occurrence or a major accident resulting in death or permanent total disability must be reported to the DOLE Regional Office within 24 hours, using the prescribed accident notification form. Dangerous occurrences include things like a boiler explosion or the collapse of a crane or hoist. The rules also make the workplace safety officer responsible for reporting accidents, disabling injuries, imminent dangers, and dangerous occurrences to the DOLE office with jurisdiction within 24 hours, so when in doubt, notify DOLE promptly.</p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">Other work accidents resulting in disabling injuries, such as a permanent partial disability or a temporary total disability, are reported to the DOLE Regional Office within 30 days after the date of occurrence, using the prescribed Work Accident/Illness Report. These incident-specific deadlines are separate from the monthly report due every 30th. The older Occupational Safety and Health Standards spoke of the 20th day of the following month, so when an incident happens, check which requirement applies and meet the earliest deadline.</p>

        <h4 style="font-size: 1.15rem; color: #2d3748; margin-top: 25px; margin-bottom: 12px;">January: Two Reports to Remember</h4>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;"><strong>13th Month Pay Compliance Report (by January 15).</strong> Employers report their compliance with the 13th month pay requirement to DOLE by January 15 of the following year. Presidential Decree No. 851 creates the benefit itself, while DOLE issuances govern the reporting. Remember that this reporting date is separate from the deadline for actually paying the benefit, which is on or before December 24.</p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;"><strong>Annual Work Accident/Illness Exposure Data Report (by January 30).</strong> This yearly summary covers your workforce, the total hours worked, and the work-related accidents and illnesses for the year. The revised safety rules require it by January 30 of the following year, even if no accident or illness occurred. Use the prescribed form and make sure your figures match your payroll, attendance, and safety records.</p>

        <h4 style="font-size: 1.15rem; color: #2d3748; margin-top: 25px; margin-bottom: 12px;">March 31: The Annual Medical Report</h4>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">This report summarizes the occupational health services and medical activities of your establishment during the year, such as medical examinations and clinic or first-aid arrangements. The responsible occupational health personnel, usually the company doctor or nurse, prepare it on the prescribed form, and the employer makes sure it reaches DOLE on the last day of March of the following year. The revised safety rules set the deadline, and the Occupational Safety and Health Standards (Rule 1960 on Occupational Health Services) provide the underlying health-service requirements.</p>

        <h4 style="font-size: 1.15rem; color: #2d3748; margin-top: 25px; margin-bottom: 12px;">Once a Year, On DOLE&rsquo;s Schedule: The Annual Wages Report</h4>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">Private establishments must also submit the Annual Establishment Report on Wages, a listing of rank-and-file employees and their pay, as required by Article 124 of the Labor Code. Unlike the reports above, this one has no fixed calendar date. DOLE and the National Wages and Productivity Commission announce the submission window each year. For the 2025 report, for example, the window ran from May 15 to August 31, 2026, covering pay data as of December 31, 2025.</p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">Three practical points: supervisors and managers are not included in the listing, branches file separately from the head office, and the confirmation notice you receive after submitting should be kept, because it is your proof of compliance during an inspection.</p>

        <h4 style="font-size: 1.15rem; color: #2d3748; margin-top: 25px; margin-bottom: 12px;">Safety Committee Minutes</h4>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">Employers must also organize an occupational safety and health committee, with the workplace safety officer serving as its secretary. Under Department Order No. 252, Series of 2025, the safety officer records the minutes of the committee&rsquo;s monthly meetings and submits them to DOLE.</p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">The earlier Occupational Safety and Health Standards contained additional provisions on reporting the safety organization and the safety program. Because the revised rules replaced the earlier implementing rules, those older reports should not automatically be treated as current requirements. Confirm with your DOLE Regional Office whether any separate organizational or periodic reports are still required under its current filing instructions.</p>

        <h4 style="font-size: 1.15rem; color: #2d3748; margin-top: 25px; margin-bottom: 12px;">Reports That Depend on What Happens</h4>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;"><strong>Closure, redundancy, or retrenchment.</strong> When an employer ends employment for an authorized cause covered by Article 298 of the Labor Code, such as the installation of labor-saving devices, redundancy, retrenchment, or closure of the business, written notice must generally be given to the affected employees and to DOLE at least one month before the intended date of termination. The employer must also meet the other requirements for that particular cause. Giving notice does not, by itself, make the termination lawful, but skipping it can expose an employer to liability even when the business reason is genuine.</p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;"><strong>Contractors and subcontractors.</strong> Under Department Order No. 174-17, a contractor must register with the DOLE Regional Office where it mainly operates, renew that registration, and submit a semi-annual report on its contracts, workers, and remittances. If you are the business that hires a contractor, those filings are the contractor&rsquo;s job, but they matter to you too. A contractor that is not registered is presumed to be engaged in labor-only contracting, and a finding of labor-only contracting can make you the direct employer of its workers. So before you sign, ask to see the contractor&rsquo;s DOLE certificate of registration, and keep a copy of your service agreement, because you may be asked to produce it during an inspection. Contracting in the construction industry under PCAB licensing follows separate rules.</p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;"><strong>Employing foreign nationals.</strong> Under Department Order No. 248, Series of 2025, as amended by Department Order No. 248-A, Series of 2025 and Department Order No. 248-B, Series of 2026, an employer who intends to hire a foreign national must first secure an Alien Employment Permit before the person starts work, unless the person falls under a listed exemption or exclusion, such as holders of permanent resident visas. Since June 11, 2026, DO 248-B has centralized the handling of Alien Employment Permits in the Bureau of Local Employment (BLE) of the DOLE Central Office, in place of the Regional Offices. After a permit is issued, the employer must comply with the reporting and updating requirements that come with it. Under DO 248-25, the employer submits an updated list of its foreign employees with permits, including their employment status, within 30 calendar days after each quarter ends. It also reports any change in the information given in the application within 10 calendar days, such as a new business address, a change in the employee&rsquo;s place of work, the employee&rsquo;s resignation or separation, or the closure of the business. Because of DO 248-B, these reports now go to the BLE instead of the Regional Office that issued the permit. Employers of covered establishments, meaning those registered under the Foreign Investment Act that enjoy fiscal incentives, those operating public utilities or critical infrastructure, and those identified as strategic investments, must also submit an understudy or skills development plan and progress reports on it. The reports are due every six months for permits valid for one year, and yearly for permits valid for two to three years, and they are signed by the employer, the foreign national, and the Filipino employees being trained. Foreign nationals who are exempt or excluded from the permit requirement, and owners or investors whose equity is reported to the SEC, are exempt from the training requirement. Deliberate non-compliance can bring fines of up to &#8369;10,000 per prohibited act and a bar from applying for permits for five or ten years, depending on the violation. Neither DO 248-A nor DO 248-B changes the quarterly and 10-day deadlines, but confirm the current forms and filing instructions with the BLE before you file.</p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;"><strong>Other situations.</strong> Registering your establishment with the DOLE Regional Office is also required, and certain industries have their own reporting rules. If your business operates in a specialized field, identify the specific rules before you begin.</p>

        <h4 style="font-size: 1.15rem; color: #2d3748; margin-top: 25px; margin-bottom: 12px;">Does This Apply to My Business?</h4>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">DOLE&rsquo;s revised safety rules cover workplaces in every kind of business and of every size, including those in economic zones and those that work through contractors, and the reporting provision sets no minimum headcount for the monthly accident report, the annual exposure report, or the annual medical report. Work out which reports apply to each of your workplaces, including any workplace where contractor personnel are deployed. The Annual Wages Report is required of all private establishments, with each branch filing its own. The 13th month pay report follows the coverage rules of the 13th month pay law.</p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">What changes with size is the safety setup behind the reports. The rules sort workplaces as micro (fewer than 10 workers), small (10 to 99), medium (100 to 199), and large (200 or more), and also by how hazardous the work is. These classes decide, for example, how many safety officers and health personnel you need, and what kind of safety program, clinic, or first-aid arrangement you must have. The remaining reports apply only if the situation applies to you: foreign employees, contractors, or a closure or retrenchment. If yours is a small business, ask your DOLE Regional Office how the requirements apply to your setup.</p>

        <h4 style="font-size: 1.15rem; color: #2d3748; margin-top: 25px; margin-bottom: 12px;">Why It Matters</h4>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">Missing required reports may result in findings during a DOLE inspection and corrective orders under the applicable rules, and willful failure to comply with the safety standards can draw administrative fines. Just as important, incomplete or inconsistent records can make it harder to show that you have met your underlying obligations. Filing on time is a good start, but it does not by itself prove compliance with the labor and safety standards behind the report.</p>
        <p style="margin-bottom: 15px;">The simplest habit is also the most effective: put the applicable deadlines on a shared calendar, assign responsibility for each report, and keep copies of every submission and confirmation receipt. And when a requirement is unclear, ask your DOLE Regional Office, or the office currently designated to handle the filing, before the deadline, not after.</p>
